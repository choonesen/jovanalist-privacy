Jovana List Privacy Policy

Last updated 5 September 2026

Jovana List does not collect, transmit, or store any of your personal information. Everything you type into it stays on your iPhone — with three exceptions, each of which only happens because you asked: a backup file you export yourself, a link you tap, and a link preview you asked the app to fetch. All three are described below.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no third-party SDK of any kind in the app. There is no Jovana List server: we have nowhere to receive your data and no way to ask for it.

The app makes exactly one kind of internet request, and only to a web address you typed into it yourself. It is described under Link previews below, it can be switched off, and nothing about you is sent with it.

What the app stores, and where

Your promises, shopping list, urgent items, the notes you add to any of them, the lists you make and the details on their rows, streak history, settings and counters are saved on your device only, in the app's own private storage. That data is covered by your device's protections and by your own encrypted iCloud or iTunes backups, if you make them.

A row on a list can also carry a photograph. Choosing one copies it into the app's own private storage as a file on your device; the original stays in your photo library, untouched. Jovana List asks for a picture through the standard iOS photo picker, which means it never has access to your photo library — only to the one image you hand it. Photos are never uploaded anywhere.

Notes, and links inside them

Any item can carry a note. It is ordinary text, stored on your device exactly like the item it belongs to, and it is never sent anywhere by the app.

If a note contains a web address, an e-mail address or a phone number, Jovana List shows it as a link so you can tap it. Tapping one hands the address to Safari, Mail or Phone and leaves Jovana List — it happens only when you tap, and from that point you are talking to that website, not to us. Jovana List does not fetch the page, does not check the address, and never sees anything that comes back. Link previews, above, are a separate thing and are never run on a note: the addresses in your notes are only ever read on your device, to make them tappable. Whatever the site you visit does is covered by its own privacy policy, not this one.

Nothing about a note is read or acted on except this: finding addresses in your own text so they can be tapped, on your device, as the note is drawn.

Link previews

A list made from a template can have a link on each row — a menu, a video, a book. If you put one there, the app can ask that page for its title and its thumbnail so the row shows what it is rather than a bare address.

Those requests go to the site that link belongs to, and to nobody else. There are at most three of them: the page itself; that service's own public preview API where it has one, which for a video or a song is the only place the real title is published; and whichever address the page gives for its picture, which is usually the same company's image server. They carry nothing about you: no identifier, no account, no other content from the app. They are ordinary requests for public pages, of the kind your browser makes when you open one, and what comes back — a title, a picture — is written onto that row on your device. We never see any of it. The site you are asking will see that it was asked, and — as with any request to any site — will see the network address your device is connecting from. That is exactly what it would see if you opened the link in Safari yourself, and what it does with it is covered by its own privacy policy, not this one.

It happens only for a link you put on a row yourself — either in a link field, on a list that has one, or pasted in as the row's own name, which is how a plain list gets one — and only when there is something empty on that row for it to fill. Settings ▸ Your data ▸ Link previews turns it off completely, and with it off the app makes no internet requests at all. A name that merely mentions an address is left alone, and links inside a note are never fetched — see below.

Backups you make yourself

Settings ▸ Your data ▸ Save a backup writes everything the app knows — including any photographs on your rows — into a single JSON file and hands it to the iOS share sheet. Where that file goes is entirely your choice — Files, AirDrop, a message, iCloud Drive — and it leaves the app only because you asked it to. Jovana List does not upload it, does not keep a copy, and has nowhere to send one.

The file is plain text and is not encrypted, so treat it the way you would treat a note containing the same things. Restore from a backup reads a file you pick and replaces what is on the device with it.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Jovana List schedules local reminders — the ones your iPhone creates and delivers by itself. Nothing about your tasks is sent anywhere in order to produce them, and no push server is involved. You can turn them off at any time in Settings inside the app, or in iOS Settings ▸ Notifications.

A reminder you set on a particular item shows that item's own words, so its text is handed to iOS to be displayed and will appear on your lock screen. It goes no further than your device. If you would rather it did not appear there, iOS Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ Jovana List ▸ Show Previews controls that for every app.

Deleting your data

  • In the app: Settings ▸ Your data ▸ Start over erases every promise, list, streak, counter and stored photograph immediately.
  • Deleting the app removes all of its data from your device.
  • Any backup file you exported is yours, wherever you put it. Deleting the app does not delete those, because the app never knew where they went.

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on your behalf and no request you need to send us.

Children

Jovana List does not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age, because it does not collect information at all.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever collects or transmits anything, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be described in the app's release notes.

Support and contact

This is also Jovana List' support page. For help with the app, to report something broken, or to ask about anything written here, write to hello@jovanauniverse.com.

There is no account to recover and no server that can be down: everything the app knows is on your own device, which is the point of it. If something has gone wrong, Settings ▸ Your data ▸ Save a backup will write the whole of it to a file you can keep before anything else is tried.
